While climate negotiators in Antalya spent two extra days failing to agree on anything, an AI discovery agent's answer to an energy problem — vitamin C — quietly started earning money in operating storage plants. Today's items are mostly about the same thing from opposite directions: the gap between what we decide about AI's energy appetite and what the technology is already doing to the energy system.

## Vitamin C is now a battery component, because an agent ranked it first

Shanghai AI Lab's materials agent screened hundreds of thousands of candidate substances for an iron-chromium flow-battery electrolyte additive and put ordinary vitamin C at the top of the list. Lab tests confirmed it, an industrial partner scaled it, and the formulation is now running in storage stations in Henan and Guangdong, with the project reporting +5% capacity, +3.5% stack efficiency and −20% cost. [Jiefang Daily](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-11-23/doc-inizskhn2338616.shtml) · [English corroboration](https://pandaily.com/shanghai-ai-lab-vitamin-c-flow-battery-additive-plant-use/)

The insight here isn't the molecule — it's where the bottleneck moved. Screening candidates was once the years-long part of materials discovery; now it's a batch job, and the slow steps are human lab validation and finding an industrial partner willing to change a running plant. Note also what the agent picked: ascorbic acid is cheap, unpatentable commodity chemistry. The moat in AI-suggested materials will be deployment speed, not intellectual property — which is quietly terrible news for specialty-chemicals margins and a preview of what happens in every industry where the discovery layer commoditizes.

## COP31 broke apart, and AI's electricity was on the negotiating table

The UN climate talks in Antalya closed without an adopted pact for the first time since Copenhagen in 2009 — the fossil-fuel standoff was the proximate cause, but for the first time AI data centers were a headline negotiating issue, with only a voluntary, "low-emission energy"-worded AI pledge surviving and Meta simultaneously announcing it is "re-evaluating" its net-zero timeline after a 45% year-over-year emissions rise. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/science/1040497/ai-data-centers-electricity-un-climate-summit)

The failure mode wasn't climate denial — it was arithmetic colliding. The IEA's forecast of data-centre electricity roughly doubling by 2030 landed in the same room as a draft pledge to raise electricity's share of global energy to 35% by 2035, and ministers could see that the same green electrons can't serve air conditioning, electrified transport and training runs at once. The telling casualty was Meta's net-zero "re-evaluation": the era of costless corporate climate pledges appears to have ended at precisely the moment AI made those pledges expensive, and the governance response — a toothless voluntary text — signals that no institution is currently willing to price the externality. Watch whether the EU's energy directorate, which rejected the pact as too weak, tries to do that pricing unilaterally next.

## Google connects Wallet, Drive and Gemini chats to search — the personal-context land grab

Google switched on Personal Intelligence in AI Mode with Wallet live today, meaning search answers can now be grounded in your actual purchases, files and conversations. [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-search-personal-intelligence-apps/)

Three days after Signal started treating OS-level AI agents as malware-class threats and shipping default blocks against them, Google ran the opposite play: not just allowing an AI to read your personal data, but structurally converting your financial records and private files into retrieval context. The second-order effect worth watching is that this makes personal context itself a moat — transaction histories and files are the one corpus competitors can't scrape, which turns "who do you let read your wallet" into the decade's account-system question. The agentic web is visibly forking into a permissioned stack (Google's own apps, first-party by construction) and a blocked stack (Signal, United Airlines), and the browser-shaped middle is where the fight actually happens.

## Inkling 2 Mini: the second looped frontier model in a week, and it fits on a laptop

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, an Apache-2.0 open-weights 26B-parameter MoE looped transformer — 5.4B effective active parameters, 1M-token context — that runs on consumer hardware. [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)

Twenty-four layers that behave as forty-eight by re-running the middle blocks: looping is becoming the architecture of the season, arriving twice in one week from independent labs (Z.ai's GLM-5.4 was yesterday's story). The unobvious reason looping fits *now* is that it buys depth with compute instead of parameters, which is exactly the trade a memory-bound laptop wants — the frontier is pacing-limited (yesterday's Pachocki perplexity argument) while local inference is memory-limited, and two different bottlenecks are producing two visibly different architectures. The subtle tell in the model card is that the lab itself warns the model sometimes deletes files without confirmation — the capability is arriving ahead of the sandboxing habits of the people who will run it.

## A fusion-plasma surrogate that runs in 76 microseconds and conserves mass exactly

A Los Alamos team published a latent neural-ODE surrogate for stiff collisional-radiative plasma kinetics — the fusion edge-region physics that used to take hours per simulation — which evaluates in about 76 microseconds per trajectory on a single GPU, with median absolute error of 4.2e-3 across 1,480 test trajectories and exact mass conservation built in. [arXiv 2611.27027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27027)

The design decision is the story: they projected the dynamics onto a conservative latent manifold rather than letting the network learn conservation approximately. That's the difference between a fast model and one a reactor engineer can put in a control loop — speed without the physics prior is just a confident way to be wrong at high frequency, and "hours to microseconds" is precisely the compression a fusion digital twin needs before real-time edge control is more than a slide. The same week climate talks choked on AI's electricity demand, the strongest argument for AI's energy appetite is papers like this one: the only credible path to the energy it wants to consume runs through tools that make fusion engineering iterate at software speed.
