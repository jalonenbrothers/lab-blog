The failures are migrating upstream. Two years ago an AI hallucinating a court case was a joke about a lawyer who didn't check ChatGPT; today it's a senior Crown Prosecution Service lawyer standing in front of the High Court.

## The prosecutor and the precedent that wasn't there

A senior CPS prosecutor cited *R v J [2024]* — a case that does not exist — to the High Court during a child-rape prosecution dispute, and two senior judges called her explanation "unsatisfactory" ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/03/a-serious-failure-cps-prosecutor-cites-imaginary-case-to-high-court-after-ai-search)). She says an AI search produced the citation. This is the second known CPS AI-hallucination incident after July's extradition appeal, and the Guardian now counts dozens of AI-related court errors. The uncomfortable read: the profession's anti-hallucination culture was built around *lawyers using* AI, with the defendant's liberty at stake and a duty to verify. A prosecutor citing precedent is the exact workflow judges trust most — nobody re-checks the prosecution's citations, because until now they didn't need to. Every verification norm we have assumes the incentive to fabricate sits with the accused, never with the state.

## Anthropic's S-1: eleven times the revenue, a trillion dollars of promises

The amended S-1 filed November 2 puts Anthropic's Q3 revenue at ~$17B — up roughly 1,127% year over year, with $33.24B for nine months — against a Q3 net loss of $23.29B, mostly fair-value changes on notes and derivatives, and about $895.4B in purchase commitments and leases, "substantially all" of it compute ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-ant-s-1-quarter-financials.html)). The interesting number isn't the loss, which is an accounting artifact; it's the $895B. That is a binding, multi-year, pre-committed claim on the world's chip and power supply, posted by a company whose entire margin structure assumes compute gets cheaper faster than demand grows. If that assumption breaks — a tariff, a grid delay, a fab shortfall — the commitments don't shrink. The AGI bet is no longer a research question; it's a fixed-cost covenant.

## CUA-Master: the agent flywheel, published

A 27B computer-use agent built on Qwen-3.5-27B by a CUHK/Tsinghua/HKUST-GZ/JD Explore team scores 80.2 on OSWorld-Verified — behind only GPT-6 Sol and Claude Opus 5 — using a nested double-loop that recursively improves itself from harvested real-world GUI interaction data ([arXiv 2611.03126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03126)). The same day, Microsoft's NorthCUA report shows a 33B model hitting 67.1 on OSWorld-Verified by distilling 1,400+ hours of expert task-solving trajectories ([arXiv 2611.02102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02102)). Read together, they say the moat isn't the model: it's the interaction data, and two of the week's strongest results both say you can *buy or distill* that. When frontier computer-use is a recipe applied to an open 27B backbone, the labs' remaining defense is capital and distribution — which is exactly the defense the S-1 above is priced in dollars.

## Marvell pays $115B for the part of the cluster that isn't a GPU

Marvell confirmed an all-stock acquisition of Lumentum at ~$1,132/share — a 23.9% premium on a $115B deal — to build what it calls a "category leader in AI connectivity" across electrical and optical interconnect, switching, custom silicon, and optical circuit switching ([Marvell press release](https://investor.marvell.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1040/marvell-and-lumentum-to-combine-creating-a-category-leader-in-ai-connectivity)). Marvell fell 13% and Lumentum 19% on the day. The largest semiconductor merger of this cycle is not a compute deal, and that's the whole point: at gigawatt-cluster scale, the bottleneck moves off the GPU and onto the network, and Marvell just spent a ninth of a trillion dollars saying the next decade of AI capex is an optics problem. The market's instant -13% verdict is less "wrong thesis" than "wrong price" — and if the thesis is right, the price stops mattering.

## A model that rebuilds a particle collision in 30 milliseconds

UCL's "Reconstruct Everything Model" is the first end-to-end ML system to reconstruct full particle collisions — W/Z/Higgs bosons included — straight from raw detector hits, tested on CERN Future Circular Collider simulation: reconstruction efficiency from 61% to 92%, jet resolution up 52%, W/Z discrimination up 50%, a full collision rebuilt in 30ms on a single GPU ([arXiv 2610.41889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41889)). The second-order story is the one worth keeping: it replaces a reconstruction chain that individual teams have separately optimized for decades with one learned framework. That template — raw multisensor stream in, unified model out — is portable to every instrument that currently runs a zoo of hand-tuned algorithms: telescope pipelines, seismic nets, medical imaging. The AI-in-science story of 2026 is less "AI makes discoveries" and more "AI inherits the instrument."
