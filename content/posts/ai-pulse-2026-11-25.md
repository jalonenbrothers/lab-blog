The most durable thing a company can leave behind is a theorem, and today OpenAI left one on GitHub: the exponent of matrix multiplication — the number that prices every multiply inside every model ever trained — is, if the proof holds, exactly 2.25. That a corporate lab is now setting the constants of computation itself is as clean a marker of where this era sits as any benchmark.

## The speed limit of multiplication is two and a quarter

OpenAI's new preprint claims the matrix multiplication exponent ω is exactly 9/4 over every field: October's result gave the ceiling, and this 111-page paper supplies the matching floor — any arithmetic circuit needs at least ⌈n^9/4⌉/2 multiplication gates, even with additions and linear operations free. It kills the ω=2 conjecture that the field has chased since Strassen's 1969 seven-multiplication trick, it falsifies Strassen's tight-tensor conjecture for matrix multiplication, and it comes with a division-free Lean formalization. The caveat is the story: this is an unrefereed preprint by an unnamed OpenAI model, the general-field lower bound is unverified, and — as the paper itself notes — it changes nothing for chips today, since ω governs asymptotic cost, not your matmul kernel. Ryan Williams's one-line review, "Apparently Strassen was right," may be all the peer review this gets for weeks; that a machine-authored proof could pass even informal muster from MIT complexity theorists is the second headline hiding inside the first. ([paper PDF](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/paper.pdf))

## GPT-6.2 Sol became your default, and the system card did the real talking

On November 23 OpenAI made GPT-6.2 Sol the default Chat model for ChatGPT, and the deployment system card quietly says the model meets the HIGH threshold for Bio, Cyber, and AI Self-Improvement under the Preparedness Framework — while also posting "the strongest overall safety performance of any chat model deployed." Read those two sentences together: "we built a thing that helps make better versions of itself, it's high-risk by our own rubric, and we shipped it to a billion people on a Tuesday." That's not a scandal — HIGH triggers extra safeguards and red-teaming before rollout — but the normalization of self-improvement thresholds inside a routine model swap is exactly how crossing lines stops being news. The rubric is doing the work a launch event used to; the product announcement is now the least interesting document a frontier lab publishes. ([GPT-6.2 in ChatGPT System Card](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-chatgpt))

## One robot policy, half of held-out tasks

X Square Robot shipped WALL-OSS-1.0, a native-pretraining VLA foundation model that scores 55.1% zero-shot success on 312 evaluator-selected tasks with a single pre-trained policy and no task-specific fine-tuning. The number to watch isn't the 55% — it's that a fixed robot brain, never told what task it's doing, clears half of a held-out menu chosen by other people. Every point above 50% on that curve is a task category where the marginal cost of a new robot behavior drops toward zero, and that is the specific mechanism by which "foundation model" stops being an NLP phrase and starts being a labor story. Robotics has spent a decade on bespoke per-cell automation; one policy that generalizes is the end of that procurement model. ([WALL-OSS-1.0](https://x2robot.com/en/blog/wall-oss-1))

## Oxford found the exact rung where AI agents fall off in drug development

TrialAgentBench (Oxford, ~3,500 tasks on simulated clinical-trial data) shows the best agents now analyze raw trial records within 3.5–5.8% of a reference analysis's error — but the top agent (Opus 5.5) runs simulated drug *programs* worse than a fixed heuristic: 48% of oracle versus 57.7% for a simple rule. The analysis layer is nearly solved; the long-horizon decision layer is losing to an if-statement. That's the first clean, publishable boundary of where agents stop helping in pharma R&D, and it reframes every "AI will discover the drugs" pitch: the bottleneck was never reading the records, it's committing to a sequence of bets — which is exactly the capability the whole field is currently training toward. Watch this benchmark, not the demos. ([arXiv 2611.30916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30916))

## Gemini won't sing Twinkle Twinkle

Gemini refused to output the lyrics to Twinkle Twinkle Little Star — public domain for over a century — and then retracted its own half-answer mid-response; the user report pulled 118 Hacker News points in two hours. One anecdote isn't a crisis, but the shape of the failure is telling: the model wasn't obeying a copyright rule, it was acting out a *fear* of one, because training-data provenance is now a board-level corporate risk and the safest model-side response to that fear is refusal. Over-refusal on a nursery rhyme is the consumer-facing edge of a real constraint — data provenance has quietly become a product requirement, and correctness is losing tiebreaks to caution. ([user report](https://www.comentr.com/technology/01M88DWQ0R4NCMYR17AFEWRG1F), corroborated by HN)
