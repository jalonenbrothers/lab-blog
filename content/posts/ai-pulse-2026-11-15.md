Six point five million lines of Lean proof, and the machine-code object files of Signal's encryption library underneath them — that's the image that should end this week. Everything below it, from product launches to a 90-day ultimatum, is the same story at different altitudes: the gap between what software claims and what software *is* is being mechanically closed, while the humans in the loop are quietly repricing their own judgment.

## The protocol that can no longer lie about itself

Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code formally verified Signal's libsignal at the machine-code level — 6.5 million lines of Lean proof covering PQXDH and the Triple Ratchet, forward secrecy and post-compromise security included, completed in four weeks where the manual estimate ran to years. The detail that separates this from every "AI writes proofs" headline before it: when researchers deliberately reintroduced old Signal bugs, the system correctly proved those versions *insecure*. The proof isn't decorative; it fails when it should. The deeper read: formal verification has always been priced like a cathedral — one-offs for seL4 and CompCert, forever. If four weeks is the new unit of effort, "provably correct implementation of a deployed crypto protocol" stops being a research aspiration and starts being a build step, and the billion-dollar question is whether the rest of the security industry notices before the audit firms do. ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))

## 159,041 Lovable apps, audited: the security debt of vibe-coding, now with a census

The Crissy Field audit of 159,041 apps built on Lovable — the "describe it and ship it" platform — found authentication rows that fail silently, passport details and 200K+ private messages exposed, and secrets sitting in plain sight, and it hit 757 points on Hacker News this week doing it. The insight isn't that no-code apps are insecure; that was known by everyone who ever said "MVP." It's that AI app-builders made *shipping* the free part, which moved all the cost to the part nobody watches. One-click deployment times 159,041 is a population-scale natural experiment, and the result says the vibe-coding boom ships broken auth *by default*, not by exception. If Project Zero's clock (below) is the supply side of vulnerability discovery, this is the demand side — and both sides got faster in the same week. ([Crissy Field](https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/))

## Decision models became a product category in about five days

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API: up to 1,000 yes/no/multi-choice/rubric questions scored per multimodal input in a single forward pass, 150–250 ms, $0.10 per million input tokens and zero output tokens billed — an architecture bet that most agent steps never needed text generation at all. Perplexity answered within 24 hours with open weights: Decider v1.3 (27B) and Decider Flash (4B), Apache 2.0, 91.6% query classification at $26.47 per million requests. With TypeSafe, OpenAI, and Amazon shipping decision models the same week, "classification-not-generation" hardened from a scaling trick into a market in roughly five days. The undrawn through-line comes from SO-Arena, published the same week: across 21 fast-decision models, the capability leader ranks only seventh in *reliability*, and null discrimination — noticing when the right answer isn't in the options — swings from near-chance to 0.8 while tracking accuracy only loosely. The product category is launching faster than its own quality metric exists. That's usually how commodities get made. ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/), [Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-decider), [SO-Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15777))

## Project Zero drops the extension, and the clock says why

Google's Project Zero — the team that found Spectre and Heartbleed-class bugs for a living — killed its own grace period: 90 days, hard stop, the +30-day extension gone, citing vendors that treat disclosure windows as negotiating room. The world's most elite vulnerability team formally lowering its trust in the patch pipeline is a data point about the pipeline, not the team. And the timing writes its own analysis: when AI agents are producing vulnerability-grade findings at machine rates (see the GPT-6 Cyber launch last week, and the Lovable census above), a human-paced 90+30 cycle isn't a compromise anymore — it's a queue. ([Project Zero](https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html))

## An LLM agent found a solar material that wasn't in the literature

A general-purpose LLM agent wired into Alexandria — 15 million DFT calculations, 400 million relaxed structures, a 17.4-billion-reaction graph, all open — screened solar absorbers, rejected the known candidates, identified an unreported barium–zirconium–sulfide, and wrote a lab-ready synthesis plan: precursors, crucible, atmosphere, temperatures. The experiment worth doing is the control: the *same agent* with web search only reproduces what's already published; the same infrastructure without an agent sits inert. It took both, wired together, to go past the literature. Every computational field with a decade of open data — and there are dozens — is now one plumbing project away from the same trick, which is a stranger and bigger claim than any single benchmark number. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15451))
