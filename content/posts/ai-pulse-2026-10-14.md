Another day where the frontier moved in public: two labs asked to be regulated while their rivals balked, a 400-gigabyte-scale world model landed on Hugging Face for anyone to download, and an AI agent earned a bug bounty the old-fashioned way — by finding the bug. The pace isn't slowing; it's compounding.

## OpenAI and Anthropic ask UK Parliament for binding AI rules — DeepMind and Meta refuse
In testimony to a UK parliamentary committee, OpenAI and Anthropic explicitly asked that pre-release AI safety testing be made legally binding — while DeepMind and Meta declined, and the government confirmed the current regime stays fully voluntary. It's the first open, on-the-record split inside the frontier-lab cohort on whether regulation should have teeth.
[aiincontext.com](https://aiincontext.com/articles/openai-and-anthropic-ask-uk-parliament-make-ai-safety-rules-binding-d7b19613)

## Reuters: Anthropic and OpenAI sought editorial control over what safety evaluators publish
The same two labs that just asked for binding rules spent last week, per a Reuters exclusive, negotiating contract terms that would give them significant control over what outside safety evaluators may publish — citing trade secrets. Both labs dispute the framing; Anthropic says established evaluators keep full editorial control, OpenAI says it only redacts confidential information. Independent evaluation is the industry's flagship safety mechanism — and its independence turns out to be a contract negotiation.
[postcutoff.com](https://postcutoff.com/e/2026-10-13-reuters-labs-sought-control-evaluator-publications/)

## TencentARC open-sources Multics — a world model where up to 10 players share one reality
TencentARC and HKU released Multics, the first open multi-player world model: it generates consistent first-person videos of one shared environment for up to ten simultaneous players, with overlapping viewpoints staying coherent instead of drifting apart. Weights plus Apache-2.0 training and inference code are all on Hugging Face and GitHub — multiplayer simulation has historically been the kind of thing only big-budget game engines could fake.
[Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/TencentARC/Multics) · [GitHub](https://github.com/TencentARC/Multics)

## Gemini 4 Argon goes worldwide — and its first week produced verified quantum and robotics results
Two weeks after preview, Google's frontier model became generally available on October 13 — and its early results read like a grant portfolio: a formally verified quantum subroutine with 100x lower spacetime cost, new counterexamples to a 1967 stellarator-fusion conjecture, and real-world robot task success lifted from 56% to 75%. One model, three physical domains, and the quantum result is machine-checked, not a benchmark claim.

## XBOW's AI agents earn Google's first $250K full-chain Chrome bounty of 2026
One human researcher working with XBOW's autonomous security agents (running GPT-5.6 Sol) chained together three previously unknown sandbox-escape vulnerabilities in six days — earning Google's first full-chain Chrome bug bounty of the year for an AI-assisted platform. AI is now doing offensive security at machine speed, and for once it's on the defenders' side.
[smbtech.au](https://smbtech.au/news/ai-assisted-security-platform-earns-googles-first-250000-chrome-bounty-of-2026/)

## The pace, today
A week ago the debate was whether AI agents could do vulnerability research at all; today one collected a quarter-million-dollar bounty while two labs argued about who gets to publish their safety reports. The acceleration is real — and so are the growing pains.
