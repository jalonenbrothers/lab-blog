In 2003, three physicists set up a calculation about when a hot soup of matter abruptly collapses into a black hole — and the community quietly set it aside, because doing it by hand was months of error-prone algebra. This week an agent swarm finished it in six weeks, alongside an exact answer to a second open problem, and both results landed on Hacker News's front page this morning. That's the through-line of today's edition: the systems are no longer demoing capabilities, they're depositing results — in physics, on your desktop, and unfortunately in attackers' pipelines too.

## The Calculation That Waited 23 Years

Anthropic reports that a ~6-week swarm of Claude agents solved two open problems at the heart of quantum gravity's most-studied toy models. First, it completed the 2003 Aharony et al. Hawking–Page weak-coupling calculation, showing the abrupt black-hole transition survives precisely where spacetime stops having a definite shape. Second, for the spinless modular bootstrap, it found the exact ceiling on the lightest black hole's mass — c/9.1266 — beating the best previously proven bound of c/8.5 and proving no lower ceiling exists. The bootstrap results were verified in Lean, and a physicist checked every main result; a separate physicist working with Claude reached the Hawking–Page result by a different route.

The non-obvious part isn't the physics, it's the epistemics. On the second problem, the agents spotted a numerical pattern humans had missed and imported spectral curves from an adjacent literature to close the proof — cross-domain recall is exactly the thing "AI is just interpolation" arguments said wouldn't happen. And in problem one, they did the tedious thing: the months-long hand calculation nobody wanted to do. The bottleneck in theoretical physics has quietly shifted from talent to patience, and patience is now the free resource. The caveats are real — both papers are unreviewed preprints, labelled "Primarily AI-generated text" — but this is the first time a frontier lab's flagship result and a verification-gap caveat share the same URL.

## Gemini Learns to Use Your Mac While You Keep It

Google shipped computer use in the Gemini app for Mac: the agent clicks, types, and navigates native apps and the web inside an isolated background window with a live preview and per-action approval (AI Plus/Pro/Ultra, US only for now). Google's own demo is charmingly specific — a hand-drawn sketch becomes a rigged 3D model while the user keeps their cursor. That makes it the last major lab to ship consumer computer use, and the timing pressures Apple directly: Bloomberg-spotted job listings suggest Apple's own autonomous Mac agent is targeting late 2027, roughly two years behind. Anthropic shares fell 3% after-hours on the Apple report — the agent race is now platform-strategy-tier, not a feature checklist.

The second-order read: "isolated background window" is the quiet headline. The UX bet is that agents shouldn't take over your screen at all — parallelism instead of possession. If that pattern wins, the desktop becomes a shared machine with two operators, one of whom bills by the token, and the whole "will people trust agents" question gets answered by never making people watch.

## The EU Just Confirmed Its First AI-Assisted Cyberattack

The European Commission confirmed that an intrusion detected October 22 on its internal HR systems was carried out "with AI tools" — the first time the 27-member bloc has officially reported an AI-powered attack. Coming days after Java4Shell and last week's V8 zero-day, the statement converts a trend from inference to record: AI-assisted offense is no longer a research demo or a vendor pitch, it's an incident with a spokesperson.

What's worth watching isn't the breach — HR systems, contained, disclosed — but the accounting that now becomes possible. "Done with AI tools" is a category the EU just invented for incident reports, and once one government logs it, every CERT framework on the planet has to define it too. The overused phrase "attack surface" finally gets a companion: attack supply chain.

## STORM Wins the Gordon Bell Climate Prize by Deleting a Step

At SC26 on November 19, the STORM team took the ACM Gordon Bell Climate Prize for one-step generative-AI data assimilation: a single diffusion-style model replaces the classical two-step forecast-update cycle, sustaining 6 ExaFLOPs in BF16 across Frontier's 74,400 GPUs, running 32,768-member ensembles in 34 seconds over 20 billion spatiotemporal tokens. Ensemble weather prediction, the machinery behind hurricane-track uncertainty cones, just became roughly a real-time quantity.

The interesting claim isn't the FLOPs, it's the structure: the classical pipeline wasn't sped up, it was replaced — the assimilation step and the forecast step collapsed into one learned operation. The paper explicitly argues the method ports to fusion, astrophysics, and materials, and that argument deserves attention, because "generative model as the entire inference engine" is the same architectural bet Nvidia is reportedly making on the other side of the stack with its ~$500M Inception diffusion-LLM acquisition talks. When the same idea shows up in weather forecasting and deal-making, that's a trend, not a coincidence.

## Cline-Bench: The 50% Wall, Measured Honestly

Cline released a benchmark of 47 real user coding tasks inside existing repositories, three runs per model. Gemini 4 Argon tops pass@1 at 51.1%, Opus 5.5 at 48.9% but with the best pass^3 — completing the task all three times at 42.6% — while Nemotron 3.5 Super VL collapses to 9.2% and GPT 6 Astra is the fastest of the leaders at 14.2 minutes per task.

The number that matters is pass^3, a metric that should have existed years ago: peak ability is a lottery ticket, consistency is a product. The gap between 48.9% pass@1 and 42.6% pass^3 is the honesty tax — run-to-run variance, not capability, is what makes agentic coding still feel like gambling. And ~50% on real repos, weeks after vendors' self-reported ALE scores implied near-competence everywhere, is the week's best reality-check datapoint: the frontier on actual user tasks clusters at a coin flip with variance you can see from orbit.
