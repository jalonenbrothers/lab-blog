The US labs went quiet for Thanksgiving, and the slack was picked up: in the 48 hours around the holiday, sanctioned-bound Russia and China shipped a wall of open weights — frontier-scale, permissively licensed, and downloadable by anyone. The open frontier is no longer a US/China duopoly, and today's five items are what that actually looks like.

## Sber open-sources GigaChat 4: 708B parameters, 1M-token context, MIT license

Sberbank's AI arm put the full weights of GigaChat 4 on Hugging Face under MIT — a 708B-parameter mixture-of-experts with 41B active, native one-million-token context, and a single checkpoint that switches between instant and reasoning modes per request. It reports 80.89 on SWE-bench Verified, though that's Sber's own evaluation, not an independent one. The detail worth pausing on isn't the benchmark; it's that a bank in a country under heavy compute sanctions shipped a frontier-scale, permissively licensed model complete with base and intermediate checkpoints — the training lineage anyone can now audit. Whatever export controls were supposed to prevent, they did not prevent this. ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ai-sage/GigaChat4-708B-A41B))

## Tencent's AI drug platform goes to Nature

Tencent's ORIDTP platform designed dual-target peptides from scratch — GLP-1R/GCGR co-agonists for obesity and diabetes, plus zero-shot PD-1/CTLA-4 cancer co-inhibitors — and the results were validated in mice: 94.2% tumour-growth suppression, with only 15 wet-lab tests per design round. It's the company's first Nature paper with Tencent as first author, and the generalization is the story: the same pipeline worked across two mechanistically unrelated disease classes, which is what separates a platform from a lucky molecule. Drug discovery's AI story has long been "promising molecule, call us in ten years"; this is the rarer kind — designed, synthesized, and shown to work in animals, with the code released. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11338-1))

## Open robot policies: SenseNova-RoboRSI-27B under Apache 2.0

SenseTime finished the open release of SenseNova-RoboRSI-27B, a vision-language manipulation model with Code-as-Policy control and one-command reproduction scripts, claiming zero-shot transfer to physical Piper robots. It's the third open robot foundation policy in five days, after WALL-OSS and OmniAct — the open-weights frontier is moving off the screen and into actuators. When the same week also gives us open humanoid VLA models and household humanoids entering mass production (last week's Xpeng IRON), the physical-work layer of the open ecosystem is assembling itself faster than anyone's regulation of it. ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/sensenova/SenseNova-RoboRSI-27B))

## Tencent Hunyuan's DFly2: speculative decoding that borrows the target's memory

Tencent open-sourced DFly2, a draft model for speculative decoding that reuses the target model's KV cache instead of maintaining its own — up to 4.2× speedup on Hunyuan-4-flash with unchanged outputs, working across DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.3-Flash. The unglamorous insight here is where the industry's actual capability-per-dollar gains are coming from: not from bigger weights, but from tricks that make the same weights cheaper to run. A draft model that shares its parent's cache is the highest-leverage trick in the inference stack, and it's now public code. ([Epoch Zero](https://epoch0.tokyo/daily/2026-11-27/en))

## GPT-6.2 Astra solves a 218-year-old mystery in 22 minutes

An anonymous 1808 sketchbook in Yale's Beinecke archive has defied attribution for two centuries. GPT-6.2 Astra, given the problem, formed a hypothesis, predicted that a specific unexamined fur-trade ledger would confirm it, went and found the ledger, and was right — in 22 minutes. It hit HN's front page this morning (407 points). The reason this belongs next to the peptides and the weights is the *type* of reasoning: archival research is non-deterministic, evidence-weighted, and historically the least automatable kind of humanities work. AI-driven discovery is no longer confined to places with clean rules; the archive, of all places, just became a target domain. ([Generative History](https://generativehistory.substack.com/p/gpt-62-astra-solves-a-historical))
