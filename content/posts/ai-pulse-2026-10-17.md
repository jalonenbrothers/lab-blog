Twenty-six years, one nagging question: were the "nothing up my sleeve" constants in NIST's elliptical curves actually clean? This week an AI answered it — and that's not even the biggest story of a Friday that saw the open-weights frontier arrive in earnest.

## Open weights hit frontier scale — twice in 48 hours
Reflection AI shipped the weights of [Beam](https://reflection.ai/blog/beam-is-available), a 501B-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts (23B active) with a 1M-token context under Apache 2.0 — the first release from the Nvidia-backed lab, and already #2 among US open-weight models on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. Within a day, Meta answered with [Muse Spark Open](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open): 816B parameters (42B active), natively multimodal, co-optimized with Apple to run on M5 Ultra Mac Studios — billed as the most capable US open-weight model to date, though its Muse Model License carries commercial limits. Two days after [StepFun's Step 5 Preview](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/Step-5-Preview) and [NVIDIA's local-agent Nemotron](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/local-ai-rtx-spark-laptops-software-updates/), the open race is no longer a sidebar of distilled small models; it's US-vs-US-vs-China at frontier scale, and anyone with the hardware can now download the argument.

## China proposes export controls — on its own models
Beijing's Ministry of Commerce floated a draft export-control catalogue adding 40 technologies, 23 of them AI-specific: large language models, autonomous agents, chip designs, and compute clusters, with comments open until November 15 ([Tech Insider, citing Bloomberg/Reuters](https://tech-insider.org/china-ai-export-curbs-23-technologies-2026/)). If adopted, Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM, and Kimi weights would be treated the way Washington treats Nvidia chips — which lands with particular force the same week US labs dropped two frontier-scale open models. The global open-weight economy may be about to split in two, and that renegotiation matters more than any single benchmark this cycle.

## Anthropic opens its books on hiring
Frontier labs rarely publish internal employment data, so [this Anthropic post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic) is a rare artifact: junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9% of intake in roughly 18 months, agents logged 6 hours for every 1 employee hour by August 2026, and engineers ship about 5x more commits — with human review bandwidth now named as the production bottleneck. It's one lab's self-report, not a labor-market study, but it's the first hard inside numbers confirming that junior-role compression is real where AI usage is heaviest.

## Claude traces the NIST curve seeds — mystery closed, no backdoor
Since 1999, cryptographers have side-eyed the seed values behind NIST's P-192 through P-521 elliptical curves, the math under TLS and much of the internet's key exchange. Anthropic reports that [Claude traced them to timestamp-seeded lrand48 output](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) — banal RNW, no backdoor — in under 30 minutes, without being pointed at the clue, and the result is checkable by anyone running the published code. The unglamorous answer is exactly what makes this a landmark: a 26-year-old integrity question in cryptography, closed by a model and verifiable in an afternoon.

## Four agents, one Ender Dragon, no humans
Google DeepMind reports that [four Gemini 4 Argon agents beat Minecraft from a random seed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319): Ender Dragon down after 27h14m, 2783 self-written and self-debugged programs, 605 deaths, coordinating purely through in-game chat and shared memory — no hand-written tools, no pre-scouted worlds. It's a game, but it's the cleanest public end-to-end measure yet of multi-agent long-horizon autonomy: planning, division of labor, and self-recovery across a 27-hour task with nobody watching.

A week that opens with two frontier-scale open models, and closes with a state proposing to fence its own weights in, tells you where the leverage now sits: capability is spreading faster than the rules being written for it.
