Three weeks ago an AI-assisted Riemann claim was the wildest thing on the internet; today Scott Aaronson can post "HOLY SHIT" four times in one essay and have it read as a sober week-in-review. That's the frame for today: the week AI stopped proving party tricks and started moving results that date back to the 1990s, the 1960s, and the foundations of RSA itself.

## Google's agent system moves worst-case factoring for the first time since 1992

Google's Cogentic — a multi-agent math research system built on Gemini 4 — dropped six papers in a single day, including an unconditional integer-factorization algorithm running in L_N[1/3, O(1)] time. That is the first rigorous improvement on the Lenstra–Pomerance L[1/2,1] bound that has stood since 1992, and Lenstra–Pomerance is precisely the result RSA's worst-case safety margin leans on. [google-research/cogentic_research_papers](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/cogentic_research_papers)

The non-obvious part isn't the exponent — it's that this is now a *race with three horses*. Anthropic's Lean proofs, OpenAI's nine-manuscript drop on Tuesday, and now Google shipping agent-authored factorization results means no single lab's safety claims bound the field anymore. The Lean formalizations are still pending, so treat the constants as provisional, but the structural shift — labs shipping proof-drops the way they ship model checkpoints — is already here.

## OpenAI closes a 60-year question — by proving where the floor is

A separate OpenAI preprint shows the Fourier transform needs only N(log N)^{1/2+o(1)} linear gates *and* proves no algorithm can do better in that model. Faster FFTs are routine; a **matching lower bound** on a computation inside essentially every signal, image, and audio pipeline is not. It also refutes the classic Ω(N log N) lower bound for unrestricted models, which every algorithms textbook has repeated since the 1960s. [The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms (PDF)](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/paper.pdf)

The insight worth keeping: the same nine-manuscript drop also disproved the polynomial Hirsch conjecture, the 60-year-old guess that bounds how badly the simplex method can behave on linear programs. Both are *answers to questions humans kept asking because we didn't know the terrain* — one upper bound, one lower bound, one counterexample. Independent developers kernel-checked the 8,115-file Lean proof on their own Macs, which tells you verification, not generation, is now the scarce resource. When Aaronson says AI-assisted complexity proofs are becoming unremarkable, he means the *checking* got cheap enough to be ambient.

## Agents now beat domain experts on open research problems — with time to spare

The REE benchmark put frontier agents on 100 expert-reviewed, *open-ended* research problems — not benchmark trivia, questions where improving the current best solution is an active research task. Claude Opus 5.5 beat the expert solutions on 86% of them and GPT-6.1 Sol on 81%, both within 12-hour budgets, and both typically surpassing the expert answer within about two hours. [arXiv 2611.34646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34646)

This is the datapoint the "agents can't do real work" discourse will have to route around: the same week SemiAnalysis's GDPx showed no agent above 12% on *professional* work tasks, REE shows them beating *experts* on research problems. The reconciliation is uncomfortable rather than contradictory — agents excel exactly where the problem has a checkable answer and struggle where success means negotiating a messy human environment. Expect the frontier to attack exactly that gap: the mundane-professional-work bottleneck, not the research ceiling.

## Cloudflare quietly invents the agent-web's robots.txt

Cloudflare provisioned a `/.well-known/search` endpoint on every domain it fronts — a standard path agents can query that returns *retrieved source content in Markdown*, not a generated answer. It's infrastructure from a company that runs ~20% of the web, which means it could become a de facto standard by default rather than by committee. [Cloudflare: Search for Agents](https://blog.cloudflare.com/search-for-agents/)

The tell is what Cloudflare chose to return: sources, not synthesis. After a month of AI reading private Signal previews and airlines blocking agents outright, the industry's first instinct at scale is a protocol that keeps generation *client-side* — give agents the text and let their model do the summarizing, so the site never vouches for an answer it didn't write. Whether that restraint survives contact with ad-driven business models is the open question; the same companies that want agent traffic also want to know it's coming.

## OpenAI claws capability back at the serving layer

An OpenAI engineer confirmed that GPT-6.2 usage is being cut: Astra now hits rate limits 40% faster, Sol 25% faster. [Thomas Sottiaux (X)](https://twitter.com/thsottiaux/status/2125784147889393806)

Read this against the same model family's system card, which this week said Sol hits a "High" AI-self-improvement threshold — the first released model to do so. The capability frontier is advancing fast enough that *serving it* has become the cost center, and the knob OpenAI is turning is how much of the model you get to use per hour, not what the model can do. Usability is now an economic decision, which means the benchmark numbers reviewers quote and the experience subscribers get are officially different quantities — expect eval-vs-experience gaps to become a standing complaint, not a bug.

Watch tomorrow for whether the Lean community certifies the factoring formalization as fast as it did Alpöge's Riemann tarball.
