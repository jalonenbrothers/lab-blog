What happens when the interesting question stops being "which model is smartest" and becomes "which system delegates best"? Today's crop leans hard toward that answer, with agent managers, AI-designed enzymes and a metal-turned-battery all landing within 72 hours of each other.

## OpenAI ships GPT-5.7-Business with Orchestration — an agent that runs other models

OpenAI's newest business release is less a chatbot than a foreman: a 10B "worker agent" that plans, executes code, browses, and — the new bit — can invoke other models as sub-tools. Beta customers reportedly compressed task pipelines from 19 steps to 5. The economics quietly shift here: if a manager can route subtasks to whatever model is best (or cheapest) per step, owning the top of the delegation stack matters more than owning every worker beneath it. That's a job-market story too — the premium moves from doing the task to specifying and checking it.

## MIT's model gives AI a "crystal clear" 3D view of materials science

MIT researchers built a model that fuses 3D atomic structures with the natural-language descriptions scattered across crystallography papers and databases, letting language models reason about crystal properties and nominate candidate materials for a given application. Materials discovery has always been bottlenecked by fragmented literature — the useful facts exist, but not in one queryable place. This turns that pile into a design space, the same move that earlier gave AI control of protein structures.

[MIT News — Giving AI a crystal-clear view of the materials world](https://news.mit.edu/2026/giving-ai-crystal-clear-view-of-materials-world-1018)

## AI-designed proteases cut disease proteins with 10-billion-fold precision

A de novo zinc-dependent protease designed by AI — an enzyme family never seen in nature — cleaves the disease-linked proteins TDP-43 and amyloid-beta with rate enhancements up to 10 billion-fold. Both targets sit at the heart of ALS and Alzheimer's pathology, which makes this less "lab curiosity" and more "programmable therapeutic modality, if it survives trials." The sober read: rate enhancement in vitro is not a drug, and delivery, specificity and immunogenicity all remain unsolved. Still, enzymes you can specify by target are a category the field didn't have a few years ago.

[Bioengineer — AI-designed enzymes slice disease proteins](https://bioengineer.org/ai-designed-enzymes-slice-disease-proteins-with-unprecedented-precision/)

## Machine learning finds the additive that turns zinc into a hydrogen battery

A Tsinghua team used ML-guided screening to identify lactate as a zinc-binding ligand, doubling dissolved Zn(II) to 1.12 M and making plain zinc a rechargeable hydrogen carrier (Nature Synthesis). Hydrogen logistics normally means compressors or pipelines; a liquid you can truck at ambient pressure would change renewable-energy transport math considerably. The AI's role was the boring-but-decisive one: screening candidate ligands computationally instead of one lab test at a time.

[Scienmag — Zinc chemistry breakthrough turns metal into a reusable hydrogen battery](https://scienmag.com/zinc-chemistry-breakthrough-turns-metal-into-a-reusable-hydrogen-battery/)
