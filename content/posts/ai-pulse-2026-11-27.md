The month's running theme keeps sharpening: the frontier's hard part is no longer writing the thing, it's proving the thing. Today five stories land on the same line from different directions — an API rewrite, a new identity layer for agents, a generalist model in a wet lab, humanoids on a car line, and machine-speed defense for running software.

## Anthropic rewrote its API in Rust. The rewrite was the easy part.

Anthropic migrated the Claude Messages API from Python to Rust with zero downtime — ~70% less CPU, first token ~20% faster — but the numbers aren't why this story leads. The content is the validation method: months of shadow traffic where match rate climbed from 24% to 99.6%, and a Claude-agent audit of the migration that caught more than 100 test assertions the rewrite had quietly weakened. Think about what that means for the industry's employment anxieties: the safest job on a rewrite like this isn't writing code, it's interrogating the code that was written. [Anthropic Research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust)

## Google makes agents identify themselves — and bills them differently.

From Feb 1, 2027, AI agents reaching Workspace data must go through MCP servers or the CLI instead of raw REST APIs, with OAuth clients forced to declare `is_agentic` and a separate billing tier from April. This is the first major platform to make agent traffic its own class — identifiable, gateable, priced apart. The quiet story here is jurisdictional: once agents carry an explicit identity bit, every platform can write policy against them, which is exactly what United Airlines, Signal, and the agentic-payments crowd have been improvising on their own. Google just standardized the pattern at the productivity-suite scale. [Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/mcp-servers-cli-standardized-usage-tiers-now-available.html)

## GPT-6 designs proteins with reasoning and plain code.

OpenAI reports GPT-6 models designing protein structures and sequences using only their own reasoning and general-purpose coding — no RFdiffusion-style specialist stack in the design loop. Wet-lab partner Onava built 322 of the designs: 35 bound their targets across all five targets, and ~70% of redesigned antibody loops kept binding. The honest caveats are in the preprint and worth keeping: scoring is OpenAI's own, nothing is refereed, and full de novo structure prediction still fails (TM 0.38 vs ESMFold2's 0.93). Our read: the significance isn't "LLM beats bespoke bio models" — it's that the generalists have a plausible path into territory everyone assumed would stay specialist forever, which changes what a drug-discovery lab hires for before it changes what it ships. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities/) · [independent recap](https://aitoolsrecap.com/Blog/openai-gpt-6-protein-design-lab-results)

## Volvo puts humanoids on a real line, with dates.

Volvo Cars has begun training Hexagon's AEON wheeled humanoids (1.65 m, 60 kg, 10 kg payload) on live tasks at its Ghent plant — imitation learning on fixture placement, factory pilot in H1 2027, production use from mid-2027. After AGCO's Kansas commitment, BMW, and Renault, the robotics wave of this month's sim-RL posts now has a European OEM with a calendar, not a concept video. The detail that matters is the task choice: fixture placement on tasks where fixed arms can't reach — humanoids sold on ergonomics, not on anthropomorphism. [Hexagon Robotics](https://robotics.hexagon.com/volvo-cars-aeon-humanoid-robots-for-manufacturing/)

## Shirudo: an AI-written shield for software that's still running.

OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, an open-source eBPF runtime that blocks exploit paths on live Linux systems while patches roll out — demonstrated blocking a Java4Shell attempt against a running Elasticsearch with no restart. This is the counterweight to a month of AI-offense headlines (V8 in 15 minutes, patch-diff-to-exploit pipelines): defense at machine time constants too, and this one's generated, not hand-written. The asymmetry story now cuts both ways, which is not something anyone could say credibly a year ago. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo/)
